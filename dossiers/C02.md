# C2: Short GRBs and neutron star mergers

**Defining problem.** Establishing that short-duration gamma-ray bursts are produced by the coalescence of compact-object binaries — double neutron stars or neutron star–black hole systems — and working out everything that follows from that identification: the r-process nucleosynthesis and kilonova emission from the merger ejecta [1989Natur.340..126E, 2010MNRAS.406.2650M], the gravitational-wave counterpart [1992ApJ...395L..83N, 2017PhRvL.119p1101A], the host-galaxy demographics that distinguish mergers from massive-star collapse [2005Natur.437..851G, 2005Natur.437..845F], and the nature of the post-merger remnant and its jet [2017ApJ...850L..19M, 2018Natur.561..355M]. The community was founded on theory: Eichler et al. argued in 1989 that coalescing neutron stars should produce gravitational waves, r-process elements, and a subclass of observable gamma-ray bursts simultaneously [1989Natur.340..126E], building on the cosmological-distance hypothesis of Paczynski [1986ApJ...308L..43P, 1991AcA....41..257P]; the identification of the short/hard burst class itself came from BATSE duration bimodality [1993ApJ...413L.101K]. The defining prediction — a gamma-ray burst accompanying a binary-neutron-star gravitational-wave chirp [1992ApJ...395L..83N] — was confirmed 25 years later by GW170817/GRB 170817A, detected 1.7 s after coalescence at 40 Mpc [2017PhRvL.119p1101A, 2017ApJ...848L..12A].

## Populations and events

- Short/hard GRBs (duration < 2 s, harder spectra), identified as a distinct class in the first BATSE catalog [1993ApJ...413L.101K]
- GRB 050509B — first rapid arcsecond localization of a short GRB, associated with an elliptical galaxy at z=0.225 in a cluster, 40+/-13 kpc offset, no supernova [2005Natur.437..851G, 2006ApJ...638..354B]
- GRB 050709 — first optical afterglow of a short burst, in the outskirts of a star-forming dwarf at z=0.160, supernova definitively excluded [2005Natur.437..845F, 2005Natur.437..859H]
- GRB 060614 — a ~102 s burst with no supernova to deep limits but short-like temporal lag and peak luminosity, breaking the two-class scheme [2006Natur.444.1044G]
- GRB 130603B — first claimed kilonova, near-infrared excess interpreted as r-process radioactive decay [2013Natur.500..547T]
- GW170817 / GRB 170817A / AT 2017gfo — the multi-messenger binary neutron star merger: SNR 32.4 chirp, total mass 2.74 Msun, GRB 1.7 s later, kilonova, and delayed X-ray/radio afterglow [2017PhRvL.119p1101A, 2017ApJ...848L..12A]
- The SGR 1806-20 giant flare of 2004, which from a great distance would appear as a short hard GRB [2005Natur.434.1098H]
- Double neutron star and NS-BH binaries as gravitational-wave sources for LIGO [1992ApJ...395L..83N, 1992ApJ...400..175B]

## Instruments and datasets

- CGRO/BATSE burst catalogs (duration-hardness classification) [1993ApJ...413L.101K]
- Swift BAT/XRT rapid localizations of short bursts [2005Natur.437..851G, 2006ApJ...638..354B]
- Advanced LIGO and Advanced Virgo gravitational-wave detectors [2017PhRvL.119p1101A]
- Fermi-GBM (detection of GRB 170817A) [2017PhRvL.119p1101A, 2017ApJ...848L..12A]
- Ground-based optical/NIR follow-up telescopes and HST for afterglows, hosts, and kilonovae [2005Natur.437..859H, 2013Natur.500..547T]
- Radio facilities and VLBI imaging of the GW170817 afterglow [2018Natur.554..207M, 2018Natur.561..355M]
- Chandra/XMM X-ray monitoring of GW170817 [2019MNRAS.483.1912P]

## Characteristic methods

- Duration/hardness distribution analysis for burst classification [1993ApJ...413L.101K]
- Rapid multi-wavelength afterglow localization and host-galaxy association by positional coincidence [2005Natur.437..851G, 2006ApJ...638..354B]
- Deep optical imaging to include or exclude an accompanying supernova [2005Natur.437..845F, 2006Natur.444.1044G]
- Analytic modeling of tidal interaction, mass loss, and fireball formation in inspiraling compact binaries [1992ApJ...400..175B, 1992ApJ...397..570M]
- Radioactive-decay light-curve modeling with nuclear reaction networks and radiative transfer (kilonova models) [2010MNRAS.406.2650M]
- Gravitational-wave matched-filter detection and parameter estimation [2017PhRvL.119p1101A]
- Joint multi-messenger inference combining GW masses with electromagnetic energetics to constrain the remnant and equation of state [2017ApJ...850L..19M]
- VLBI proper-motion measurement of merger afterglows [2018Natur.561..355M]
- Monte Carlo population synthesis of merger delay-time distributions against redshift samples [2011ApJ...727..109V]

## Recurring models

- Compact binary coalescence (NS-NS / NS-BH) as the short-GRB central engine, with a post-merger accretion disk or torus [1989Natur.340..126E, 1991AcA....41..257P, 1992ApJ...395L..83N]
- The ultrarelativistic e+e- fireball, necessarily anisotropic to escape the neutron-rich ejecta [1986ApJ...308L..43P, 1992ApJ...397..570M, 1992ApJ...395L..83N]
- The kilonova: quasi-isotropic transient powered by radioactive decay of r-process nuclei in ~10^-2 Msun of ejecta, with lanthanide opacity pushing emission to the near-infrared [2010MNRAS.406.2650M, 2013Natur.500..547T]
- Neutron star mergers as a dominant r-process nucleosynthesis site [1989Natur.340..126E, 2013Natur.500..547T]
- The structured/off-axis relativistic jet and its cocoon for GRB 170817A's faint prompt and slowly rising afterglow emission [2018Natur.554..207M, 2018Natur.561..355M]
- Magnetar alternatives: extragalactic SGR giant flares for a fraction of short bursts, and long-lived magnetized neutron star remnants [2005Natur.434.1098H, 2019MNRAS.483.1912P]
- Two-class (later Type I/Type II) burst taxonomy anchored at the 2 s duration threshold [1993ApJ...413L.101K, 2006Natur.444.1044G, 2011ApJ...727..109V]

## Results that shaped it

- Eichler et al. 1989: coalescing neutron stars should jointly produce gravitational-wave bursts, r-process elements, and a subclass of gamma-ray bursts, with consistent rates [1989Natur.340..126E]
- Paczynski's cosmological hypothesis: bursts at z~1 requiring ~10^51 erg and relativistic e+e- outflow, with a BH-NS binary decay as the engine [1986ApJ...308L..43P, 1991AcA....41..257P]
- Kouveliotou et al. 1993: bimodal BATSE duration distribution splitting GRBs into short/hard and long/soft classes at 2 s [1993ApJ...413L.101K]
- Narayan, Paczynski & Piran 1992: merger-powered cosmological fireballs, with the prediction that GRBs are accompanied by LIGO-detectable gravitational radiation [1992ApJ...395L..83N]
- Meszaros & Rees 1992: neutron-rich baryonic pollution from tidal mass loss forces merger GRB models to be anisotropic [1992ApJ...397..570M]
- Bildsten & Cutler 1992: inspiraling neutron stars cannot be tidally locked, and NS-BH mass transfer is unstable [1992ApJ...400..175B]
- The 2005 Swift/HETE-2 breakthrough: GRB 050509B localized to an elliptical galaxy and GRB 050709's optical afterglow with no supernova, ruling out young-population progenitors and favoring coalescing degenerate binaries [2005Natur.437..851G, 2006ApJ...638..354B, 2005Natur.437..845F, 2005Natur.437..859H]
- Metzger et al. 2010: quantitative kilonova light curves peaking in ~1 day at ~10^41.5 erg/s for 10^-2 Msun of r-process ejecta [2010MNRAS.406.2650M]
- Tanvir et al. 2013: NIR kilonova candidate accompanying GRB 130603B, first direct evidence that mergers make r-process elements [2013Natur.500..547T]
- GW170817/GRB 170817A 2017: first direct evidence linking binary neutron star mergers to short GRBs, with the full multi-messenger dataset including kilonova AT 2017gfo [2017PhRvL.119p1101A, 2017ApJ...848L..12A]
- Margalit & Metzger 2017: multi-messenger constraint M_max <~ 2.17 Msun on the neutron star maximum mass from GW170817 [2017ApJ...850L..19M]
- Mooley et al. 2018: VLBI superluminal motion proving GW170817 launched a successful narrow (<5 deg) relativistic jet viewed ~20 deg off-axis [2018Natur.561..355M]

## Internal disagreements

- Geometry of the GW170817 outflow: radio light-curve modeling argued there was no signature of an off-axis jet and that a mildly relativistic wide-angle cocoon explained the gamma-ray through radio data [2018Natur.554..207M], whereas subsequent VLBI imaging of superluminal apparent motion broke the choked- vs successful-jet degeneracy and showed the late emission was dominated by an energetic, narrowly collimated (<5 deg) jet [2018Natur.561..355M].
- Fate of the GW170817 merger remnant: Margalit & Metzger used the kilonova energetics to argue the remnant collapsed quickly, excluding a long-lived supramassive neutron star and setting M_max <~ 2.17 Msun [2017ApJ...850L..19M], while Piro et al. argued a long-lived magnetized neutron star remnant with GW-dominated spin-down is fully consistent with the electromagnetic dataset, pointing to a ~3-sigma X-ray feature at 155 d as possible central-engine reactivation [2019MNRAS.483.1912P].
- Purity of the merger-progenitor hypothesis: the 2005 afterglow discoveries argued young-population models (magnetars, collapsars) are ruled out and coalescing degenerate binaries are the most promising progenitors [2005Natur.437..845F], but Hurley et al. argued from the SGR 1806-20 giant flare that a significant fraction of short GRBs may be extragalactic magnetar flares [2005Natur.434.1098H], and Virgili et al. found that merger models alone cannot reconcile the redshift and flux distributions, requiring either a component tracking star formation or an atypical ~2 Gyr delay [2011ApJ...727..109V].
- Validity of the 2 s two-class taxonomy: the BATSE bimodality established short vs long classes at 2 s [1993ApJ...413L.101K], but GRB 060614 — a ~102 s burst with no supernova to deep limits and short-like lag and luminosity — fits neither class, prompting calls for a new classification scheme [2006Natur.444.1044G].

## Activity

Founded by theory papers of 1986-1993 [1986ApJ...308L..43P, 1989Natur.340..126E, 1992ApJ...395L..83N, 1993ApJ...413L.101K], with a first observational surge in 2005-2006 when Swift localized short bursts to their hosts [2005Natur.437..851G, 2006ApJ...638..354B]. The dominant burst of activity follows GW170817 in 2017 — reflected in the community's median year of 2019 and the very heavily cited 2017-2018 multi-messenger papers [2017PhRvL.119p1101A, 2017ApJ...848L..12A, 2018Natur.561..355M]. It remains active into the 2020s on remnant physics, jet structure, and equation-of-state constraints [2019MNRAS.483.1912P], now one of the largest and most current sub-literatures of GRB research (2009 papers).
