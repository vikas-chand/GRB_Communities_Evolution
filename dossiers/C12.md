# C12: Terrestrial and astrobiological effects of GRBs

**Defining problem.** What a Galactic gamma-ray burst would do to the Earth (and to habitable planets generally): how often a burst close enough to matter occurs, how its ionizing photons deposit energy in an atmosphere, how the resulting NOx chemistry depletes ozone and admits biologically lethal solar UVB, and whether any event in the geological or historical record — above all the Late Ordovician mass extinction ~440-445 Myr ago and the AD 774/5 radiocarbon spike — can be attributed to one. The founding paper [1995ApJ...444L..53T] posed the problem the moment BATSE made GRBs cosmological: if bursts occur in normal galaxies, one strikes our Galaxy every 1e5-1e6 yr, its gamma-ray flux dwarfing the largest solar flares, driving stratospheric NO production, multi-year ozone loss, and possibly anomalous radionuclide traces. The community then quantified the rate per galaxy [2002ApJ...566..723S], the atmospheric response in detail [2005ApJ...622L.153T; 2005ApJ...634..509T; 2005GeoRL..3214808M; 2007ApJ...654..373E], the Ordovician-extinction hypothesis [2004IJAsB...3...55M; 2009Pbio...35..311M], and the generalization to exoplanet atmospheres, the Galactic Habitable Zone, and the Fermi paradox [2004Icar..171..229S; 1999JBIS...52...19A; 2014PhRvL.113w1102P; 2021A&A...647A..41S].

## Populations and events

- Hypothetical nearby Galactic GRBs within ~2 kpc of Earth, estimated to occur at least once per Gyr [2005ApJ...622L.153T], or two or more dangerously near events per Gyr [2004IJAsB...3...55M]
- The Late Ordovician mass extinction (~440-445 Myr ago), the community's flagship candidate GRB-initiated extinction [2004IJAsB...3...55M; 2005ApJ...634..509T; 2009Pbio...35..311M]
- The AD 774/5 rapid 14C increase in tree rings (7e24 erg gamma-ray energy input at Earth), attributed to a Galactic short GRB at 1-4 kpc [2013MNRAS.430...32H] or to a solar particle event [2013NatCo...4.1748M; 2018NatCo...9.3495U]
- The second rapid 14C event of AD 992-993 with matching 10Be signature [2013NatCo...4.1748M]
- Low-metallicity long-GRB host galaxies at z<=0.25, all with 12+log(O/H)<8.6 [2006AcA....56..333S]
- Short-hard GRBs as a local threat comparable to supernovae and exceeding long bursts [2011AsBio..11..343M; 2007ApJ...654..373E]
- Terrestrial-like exoplanets in the habitable zone, receiving GRB/SN fluences of 1e6-1e9 erg/cm^2 at ~100 events per Gyr [2004Icar..171..229S]
- The Galactic-position-dependent population of habitable planets (Galactic Habitable Zone) [2014PhRvL.113w1102P; 2021A&A...647A..41S]

## Instruments and datasets

- BATSE/CGRO isotropy and intensity distributions, the observational trigger for the founding cosmological-distance argument [1995ApJ...444L..53T]
- Local GRB rate and peak-luminosity estimates (Schmidt) combined with cosmic star-formation-rate history [2002ApJ...566..723S]
- Tree-ring 14C series and Antarctic ice-core 10Be flux records for AD 774/5 and AD 992/3 [2013NatCo...4.1748M; 2013MNRAS.430...32H]
- Sub-annual earlywood/latewood 14C measurements in Arctic tree rings across latitudes [2018NatCo...9.3495U]
- SDSS star-forming-galaxy census and emission-line oxygen abundances of low-z GRB hosts [2006AcA....56..333S]
- BATSE/Swift GRB luminosity functions and rate evolution used for lethality statistics [2014PhRvL.113w1102P]
- Late Ordovician paleontological geographic extinction patterns from the fossil record [2009Pbio...35..311M]
- Auger Observatory anticipated as the test of the GRB origin of ~1e18 eV cosmic rays [2005ApJ...628L..21D]

## Characteristic methods

- Atmospheric photochemistry modeling of impulsive gamma-ray energy deposition: NO/NO2 production, catalytic ozone destruction, and multi-year recovery, in 1D and 2D models [1995ApJ...444L..53T; 2005ApJ...622L.153T; 2005ApJ...634..509T; 2007ApJ...654..373E]
- Scaling cosmological GRB rates to a per-galaxy or per-blue-luminosity rate, then to the probability of a burst within a lethal distance of Earth [2002ApJ...566..723S; 2005ApJ...622L.153T; 2014PhRvL.113w1102P]
- Monte Carlo radiative transfer of ionizing photons (Compton scattering + photoabsorption) through model atmospheres of varying column density [2004Icar..171..229S]
- DNA-damage and UVB-dose estimation from computed ozone depletion, extrapolated from modern-organism sensitivity [2005ApJ...634..509T; 2005ApJ...622L.153T]
- Radiative-transfer and climate-impact estimation of NO2 visible opacity as a glaciation trigger [2005GeoRL..3214808M]
- Cosmogenic-isotope energy budgets: converting 14C and 10Be production ratios into source spectra and energies to discriminate GRB, supernova, and solar-flare origins [2013MNRAS.430...32H; 2014AN....335..949N; 2018NatCo...9.3495U]
- 3D simulations of GRB-accelerated cosmic-ray propagation in the Galactic magnetic field with pitch-angle scattering [2005ApJ...628L..21D]
- Order-of-magnitude timescale comparisons between GRB recurrence and biological-evolution timescales for Fermi-paradox arguments [1999JBIS...52...19A]
- Galactic habitability mapping: convolving GRB/SN rates, metallicity gradients, and planet occurrence over galactocentric radius and cosmic time [2014PhRvL.113w1102P; 2021A&A...647A..41S]
- Comparison of simulated hemispherical radiation-damage patterns with geographic extinction patterns in the fossil record [2009Pbio...35..311M]

## Recurring models

- The NOx-catalysis ozone-depletion model: atmospheric absorption of gamma rays dissociates N2, NO/NO2 destroys ozone for years, solar UVB reaching the surface does the biological damage — the community's central causal chain from its founding [1995ApJ...444L..53T] through the detailed calculations [2005ApJ...622L.153T; 2005ApJ...634..509T]
- The GRB-Ordovician hypothesis: a burst within ~2 kpc initiating both the UVB extinction pulse and, via NO2 opacity and cooling, the end-Ordovician glaciation [2004IJAsB...3...55M; 2005GeoRL..3214808M; 2005ApJ...634..509T]
- Fluence-scaling with duration-insensitivity: atmospheric impact depends on total fluence, nearly independent of burst duration from 0.1 s to 1e8 s, unifying short GRBs, long GRBs, X-ray flashes, and supernovae into one framework [2005ApJ...634..509T; 2007ApJ...654..373E]
- The lethal-distance / lethal-fluence model (~100 kJ/m^2 at ~2 kpc benchmark) [2005ApJ...622L.153T; 2014PhRvL.113w1102P]
- GRB regulation of the Galactic Habitable Zone and of the timing of intelligence (Fermi-paradox phase transition at mean lethal-event spacing ~1e8 yr) [1999JBIS...52...19A; 2014PhRvL.113w1102P; 2021A&A...647A..41S]
- The metallicity aversion of long GRBs (threshold ~0.15 Z_sun), which shields metal-rich galaxies like the Milky Way [2006AcA....56..333S; 2021A&A...647A..41S]
- The Galactic short GRB as cause of historical cosmogenic-isotope spikes [2013MNRAS.430...32H]
- Atmospheric reprocessing of ionizing radiation into surficial UV on exoplanets, with ~100 g/cm^2 as the gamma-ray blocking column [2004Icar..171..229S]

## Results that shaped it

- Thorsett 1995: a Galactic GRB every 1e5-1e6 yr would deliver flux far exceeding the largest solar flares, drive NO-catalyzed ozone loss for years, and possibly leave radionuclide traces — the founding statement of the field [1995ApJ...444L..53T]
- Scalo & Wheeler 2002: Milky Way GRB rate 5.5e-7/yr, >6000 SNe Ibc per isotropic-equivalent GRB, and biologically significant irradiation events at ~100-500 per Gyr for planets with thin atmospheres [2002ApJ...566..723S]
- Melott et al. 2004: the proposal that the Late Ordovician mass extinction ~440 Myr ago was at least partly GRB-initiated, with dangerously near bursts expected two or more times per Gyr [2004IJAsB...3...55M]
- Thomas et al. 2005: first detailed atmospheric computation — a 100 kJ/m^2 burst at 2 kpc gives 35% globally averaged ozone depletion (55% at some latitudes) persisting >5 yr, tripling surface UVB [2005ApJ...622L.153T], extended to 38% global / 74% local depletion, DNA damage up to 16x the annual global average, and ~7 yr recovery [2005ApJ...634..509T]
- Melott et al. 2005: NO2 visible opacity reduces surface sunlight by up to a few percent, greatest at the poles — potentially sufficient to initiate the end-Ordovician glaciation [2005GeoRL..3214808M]
- Ejzak et al. 2007: atmospheric impact is nearly duration-independent at fixed fluence (0.1 s to 1e8 s) and maximized for spectral peaks up to ~20 MeV, folding short GRBs and supernovae into the same formalism [2007ApJ...654..373E]
- Stanek et al. 2006: all five low-z long-GRB hosts have 12+log(O/H)<8.6, implying a ~0.15 Z_sun metallicity ceiling and that the metal-rich Milky Way has been unable to host long GRBs for billions of years [2006AcA....56..333S]
- Smith, Scalo & Wheeler 2004: atmospheres >~100 g/cm^2 block gamma rays but reprocess up to 10% of incident energy into surficial UV, defining the exoplanet-atmosphere response to GRB irradiation [2004Icar..171..229S]
- Hambaryan & Neuhauser 2013: the AD 774/5 14C spike (7e24 erg at Earth) is consistent with a Galactic short GRB at 1-4 kpc — claimed as the first evidence for a short GRB in our Galaxy [2013MNRAS.430...32H]
- Miyake et al. 2013: a second rapid 14C event in AD 992-993 with 14C/10Be proportions matching AD 774/5, arguing both share a common, plausibly solar, origin [2013NatCo...4.1748M]
- Uusitalo et al. 2018: latitudinal dependence of the AD 774 14C signal in Arctic tree rings is consistent with a solar particle event and inconsistent with a GRB or supernova [2018NatCo...9.3495U]
- Piran & Jimenez 2014: ~50% probability of a lethal GRB within the last 500 Myr, a 95%-lethal inner Galaxy, life confined to ~10% of galaxies, and no Earth-like life at z>0.5 [2014PhRvL.113w1102P]
- Melott & Thomas 2011 census: short-hard GRBs are a threat comparable to supernovae and greater than long bursts [2011AsBio..11..343M]

## Internal disagreements

- The cause of the AD 774/5 radiocarbon spike. Hambaryan & Neuhauser argued a Galactic short GRB at 1-4 kpc is consistent with all observables — the required 7e24 erg within a year, the hard spectrum matching the 14C/10Be production ratio, and the absence of a historical supernova sighting or remnant — calling it the first evidence for a short GRB in our Galaxy, while judging solar and stellar flares unlikely [2013MNRAS.430...32H]; Neuhauser & Hambaryan themselves quantified that an unbeamed solar flare would need ~2000x the Carrington event energy and put the probability of a sufficient super-flare within 3000 yr as low as 0.008-0.3 [2014AN....335..949N]. Against this, Miyake et al. found a second, comparable 14C event in AD 992-993 with the same 14C/10Be proportions, arguing the recurrence rate favors a solar origin over an extraterrestrial one [2013NatCo...4.1748M], and Uusitalo et al. showed the AD 774 signal has a strong poleward latitudinal dependence that a solar particle event produces but a GRB or supernova cannot [2018NatCo...9.3495U].
- Whether the Milky Way can host the long GRBs the extinction scenarios require. The Ordovician and ozone-depletion calculations assume the Galactic long-GRB rate scales from cosmological rates, giving at least one burst within 2 kpc per Gyr [2005ApJ...622L.153T; 2004IJAsB...3...55M; 1995ApJ...444L..53T]. Stanek et al. countered that long GRBs trace only low-metallicity star formation (ceiling ~0.15 Z_sun) and that the Milky Way has been too metal-rich to host them for at least the last several billion years, explicitly flagging the implication for GRB mass-extinction claims [2006AcA....56..333S]. Piran & Jimenez, assuming host-metallicity bias does not significantly alter the Galactic rate, nonetheless kept a 50% probability of a lethal GRB in the last 500 Myr [2014PhRvL.113w1102P].
- Where in the Galaxy life is safest from GRBs. Piran & Jimenez concluded the inner Milky Way (within 4 kpc of the center) has a 95% probability of lethal GRB irradiation and only regions beyond 10 kpc drop below 50% [2014PhRvL.113w1102P]; Spinelli & Ghirlanda, folding in the metallicity aversion of long GRBs and planet density, concluded the opposite geometry for the recent Galaxy — the 2-8 kpc band has been the safest for the last 4 Gyr while the low-metallicity outer Milky Way was sterilized by 2-5 long GRBs in the last 500 Myr [2021A&A...647A..41S].
- Which burst class dominates the local threat. The founding line of work centered on long/cosmological bursts and their Galactic analogs [1995ApJ...444L..53T; 2005ApJ...622L.153T], and Piran & Jimenez ranked long GRBs as the most dangerous type by energy and rate [2014PhRvL.113w1102P]; the Melott & Thomas census instead ranked short-hard bursts as a substantial threat comparable to supernovae and greater than the higher-luminosity long bursts [2011AsBio..11..343M], a shift enabled by the duration-insensitivity result of Ejzak et al. [2007ApJ...654..373E].

## Activity

Founded in 1995 with Thorsett's BATSE-era warning [1995ApJ...444L..53T], quantified in 1999-2004 [1999JBIS...52...19A; 2002ApJ...566..723S; 2004Icar..171..229S], and peaking in an intense 2004-2007 wave of Ordovician-extinction and atmospheric-modeling papers from the Melott-Thomas collaboration [2004IJAsB...3...55M; 2005ApJ...622L.153T; 2005ApJ...634..509T; 2005GeoRL..3214808M; 2007ApJ...654..373E]. A second burst of activity around 2013-2018 followed the AD 774/5 radiocarbon controversy [2013MNRAS.430...32H; 2013NatCo...4.1748M; 2014AN....335..949N; 2018NatCo...9.3495U] and the cosmological-habitability turn [2014PhRvL.113w1102P] — consistent with the community's median year of 2012.5. It remains modestly active into the 2020s, now oriented toward Galactic and exoplanetary habitability rather than new Earth-effect calculations [2021A&A...647A..41S].
