# C9: GRB Monitors and Terrestrial Gamma-ray Flashes

**Defining problem.** This community sits at the instrumental heart of GRB astronomy rather than its astrophysics: it is the literature of wide-field gamma-ray burst monitors as instruments — their localization accuracy, calibration, and detector design — together with the atmospheric-physics science those same monitors do as a byproduct, above all terrestrial gamma-ray flashes (TGFs). The founder paper quantified Fermi-GBM localization systematics, showing statistical uncertainties as small as 1 degree hide a 3.7-degree systematic Gaussian with a ~14-degree tail [2015ApJS..216...32C]. The parallel defining thread asks what produces millisecond-scale MeV flashes from thunderstorms: whether TGFs are directly tied to lightning discharges [1996GeoRL..23.1017I; 2010JGRA..11512307C], what electron-acceleration mechanism (large-scale relativistic runaway avalanche versus lightning-leader fields) generates them [2016JGRA..12111346M; 2019JGRA..124.7170M], and how a new generation of monitors (GECAM, Insight-HXMT, HERMES, GRID) can localize both cosmic GRBs and gravitational-wave counterparts [2019NIMPA.921....8Z; 2020SCPMA..6349503L; 2019NIMPA.936..199F; 2019ExA....48...77W].

## Populations and events

- Terrestrial gamma-ray flashes: 12 in Fermi-GBM's first year [2010JGRA..115.7323B], 384 with improved analysis [2013JGRA..118.3805B], 4,144 in the first GBM TGF catalog at ~800/yr [2018JGRA..123.4381R], 217 seen by ASIM in 10 months [2019JGRD..12414024O], with an inferred global rate of ~400,000/yr [2013JGRA..118.3805B]
- BATSE terrestrial gamma flashes: 13 events with ~50 microsecond variability, spectrally harder than cosmic GRBs [1997JGR...102.9659N]
- Terrestrial electron beams: >1 ms GBM events from electrons traveling along geomagnetic field lines, carrying 511 keV positron-annihilation signatures [2011GeoRL..38.2808B]
- Ground-level thundercloud gamma-ray bursts with associated photonuclear neutrons, observed at coastal Japan [2016PhLB..758..286K]
- The 300+ independently localized GRBs used to calibrate GBM systematics out of >1400 GBM detections [2015ApJS..216...32C]
- Accreting pulsars monitored by GBM/BAT, e.g. the 2008 torque reversal of 4U 1626-67 [2010ApJ...708.1500C]
- Anticipated GW-GRB coincidences: a few per year at a 200 Mpc horizon for CubeSat constellations [2019ExA....48...77W]

## Instruments and datasets

- Fermi Gamma-ray Burst Monitor (GBM) — the community's central instrument for GRB localization and TGF detection [2015ApJS..216...32C; 2010JGRA..115.7323B; 2018JGRA..123.4381R]
- CGRO/BATSE — the founding TGF and GRB-monitor dataset [1997JGR...102.9659N]
- World Wide Lightning Location Network (WWLLN) sferic data for TGF-lightning association [2010JGRA..11512307C; 2018JGRA..123.4381R]
- ELF/VLF radio atmospherics receivers (Palmer Station) and broadband 1-300 kHz magnetic-field measurements [1996GeoRL..23.1017I; 2011GeoRL..3814810C]
- GECAM twin satellites with LaBr3:Ce + SiPM gamma-ray detectors, 6 keV-5 MeV [2019NIMPA.921....8Z]
- Insight-HXMT High Energy telescope: 18 NaI/CsI phoswich detectors, ~5100 cm^2, 20-250 keV plus 0.2-3 MeV GRB monitoring [2020SCPMA..6349503L]
- HERMES nano-satellite constellation (GAGG:Ce + SDD detectors) [2019NIMPA.936..199F]
- GRID student CubeSat network, 10 keV-2 MeV [2019ExA....48...77W]
- MAXI all-sky X-ray monitor on the ISS, 0.5-30 keV [2009PASJ...61..999M]
- ASIM Modular X- and Gamma-ray Sensor on the ISS [2019JGRD..12414024O]
- Swift/BAT long-term hard X-ray monitoring [2010ApJ...708.1500C]
- PANDA 360 kg plastic-scintillator prototype for ground-level TGF detection [2016PhLB..758..286K]

## Characteristic methods

- Cross-validation of monitor localizations against independently localized GRBs (Swift, LAT, INTEGRAL, MAXI, IPN) with Gaussian modeling of systematic error distributions [2015ApJS..216...32C]
- Microsecond-accuracy temporal cross-correlation of satellite gamma-ray data with radio sferics from lightning networks [2010JGRA..11512307C; 2011GeoRL..3814810C; 1996GeoRL..23.1017I]
- Individual-TGF spectral fitting with Poisson likelihood against Monte Carlo RREA and lightning-leader templates, inferring source altitude from atmospheric absorption [2016JGRA..12111346M; 2019JGRA..124.7170M]
- Offline search algorithms, new data modes, and cosmic-ray-shower rejection to expand TGF samples tenfold [2013JGRA..118.3805B]
- Bayesian-block temporal/spectral characterization of catalog-scale TGF samples [2018JGRA..123.4381R]
- Light-travel-time arguments constraining source size from pulse risetimes [2010JGRA..115.7323B]
- Detector design, ground calibration with radioactive sources, and Geant4 background simulation for new monitors [2019NIMPA.921....8Z; 2020SCPMA..6349503L]
- Triangulation and flux-modulation localization across satellite constellations [2019NIMPA.936..199F; 2019ExA....48...77W]
- Long-term pulse timing and hardness-ratio monitoring of accreting pulsars with GBM/BAT [2010ApJ...708.1500C]

## Recurring models

- Relativistic runaway electron avalanche (RREA) in large-scale organized thundercloud electric fields as the TGF acceleration mechanism [2016JGRA..12111346M; 2016PhLB..758..286K]
- Lightning-leader-tip field acceleration (high- and low-potential leader models, often with tilted beams) as the competing TGF mechanism [2019JGRA..124.7170M]
- Bremsstrahlung from runaway electrons plus atmospheric photon propagation as the spectral forward model, making source altitude a fitted parameter [2016JGRA..12111346M; 2019JGRA..124.7170M]
- Geomagnetic-field-line transport of electron-positron beams from TGF sources to spacecraft (electron TGFs) [2011GeoRL..38.2808B; 2010JGRA..11512307C]
- Gaussian-core-plus-tail systematic error models for monitor localizations [2015ApJS..216...32C]
- Constellation triangulation with sub-microsecond timing as the localization paradigm for next-generation monitors [2019NIMPA.936..199F; 2019ExA....48...77W]

## Results that shaped it

- The first direct association of a terrestrial gamma-ray burst with a lightning discharge, via a sferic within +/-1.5 ms [1996GeoRL..23.1017I]
- BATSE TGFs vary on ~50 microsecond timescales and are spectrally much harder than cosmic GRBs, establishing them as a distinct phenomenon [1997JGR...102.9659N]
- Fermi-GBM's first TGFs: ~30 MeV maximum energies, 7 microsecond risetimes constraining source radii to <2 km [2010JGRA..115.7323B]
- 13 of 15 GBM TGF-WWLLN associations simultaneous to ~40 microseconds, tying TGFs directly to lightning discharges [2010JGRA..11512307C]
- Discovery of 511 keV annihilation lines in electron TGFs: lightning produces electron-positron pair beams injected into the near-Earth environment [2011GeoRL..38.2808B]
- New GBM data modes raised the TGF detection rate tenfold and implied ~400,000 TGFs globally per year [2013JGRA..118.3805B]
- The first Fermi-GBM TGF catalog: 4,144 events, with hard photons lagging soft by ~27 microseconds [2018JGRA..123.4381R]
- GBM GRB localizations carry a 3.7-degree systematic uncertainty with a ~14-degree non-Gaussian tail — the number that drove the push for better-localizing monitors [2015ApJS..216...32C]
- MAXI established ISS-based all-sky X-ray monitoring down to ~2 mCrab per week [2009PASJ...61..999M]
- GECAM's LaBr3:Ce/SiPM detector achieved 5.3% resolution at 662 keV over 6 keV-5 MeV, defining the post-GBM monitor design [2019NIMPA.921....8Z]
- Insight-HXMT/HE demonstrated a ~5100 cm^2 phoswich telescope doubling as a 0.2-3 MeV GRB monitor [2020SCPMA..6349503L]
- GBM/BAT long-term monitoring caught the second torque reversal of 4U 1626-67, showing GRB monitors' value for accreting-pulsar timing [2010ApJ...708.1500C]

## Internal disagreements

- TGF-lightning temporal ordering: the first GBM TGF paper reported an inconsistent sequence — one TGF before its lightning stroke, two simultaneous, one after [2010JGRA..115.7323B] — whereas the WWLLN association study found 13 of 15 associations simultaneous to ~40 microseconds and argued a large fraction of TGFs are coincident with the discharge [2010JGRA..11512307C]; microsecond magnetic-field work then split the difference, finding fast discharge-like processes with no consistent timing relationship but a slower radio component that mirrors the gamma-ray profile [2011GeoRL..3814810C]
- TGF production mechanism: individual-TGF spectroscopy concluded that narrow-beam events require large-scale RREA in organized electric fields and appear inconsistent with lightning-leader acceleration models [2016JGRA..12111346M], while a subsequent leader-model analysis of 66 bright GBM TGFs found 59% best fit by high-potential leader models and concluded spectral analysis alone cannot strongly discriminate between the mechanisms [2019JGRA..124.7170M]
- TGF source altitude and the sprite connection: the founding papers speculated TGFs were tied to high-altitude phenomena, with slow-tail sferics resembling sprite-associated discharges [1996GeoRL..23.1017I] and BATSE timing argued to favor a red-sprite over blue-jet relation [1997JGR...102.9659N]; later spectral fits placed TGF sources inside thunderclouds at 11.6-20.2 km [2016JGRA..12111346M] and mostly below 15 km [2019JGRA..124.7170M], displacing the sprite-altitude picture

## Activity

Founded in the BATSE era (1996-1997) with the TGF-lightning association papers [1996GeoRL..23.1017I; 1997JGR...102.9659N], the community's first boom came with Fermi-GBM TGF science in 2010-2013 [2010JGRA..115.7323B; 2013JGRA..118.3805B], matured into catalogs by 2018-2019 [2018JGRA..123.4381R; 2019JGRD..12414024O], and then pivoted toward next-generation monitor instrumentation (GECAM, HXMT, HERMES, GRID) in 2019-2022 [2019NIMPA.921....8Z; 2020SCPMA..6349503L; 2019NIMPA.936..199F; 2019ExA....48...77W]. With a median year of 2021, it is currently active, dominated by the instrument-paper wave for GW-counterpart monitors rather than by new TGF physics.
