# C5: Collapsar and GRB-supernova connection

**Defining problem.** What stellar systems and central engines produce gamma-ray bursts — specifically, whether long GRBs come from the collapse of massive stars whose cores form a black hole plus hyperaccreting disk ("failed supernova"/collapsar) [1993ApJ...405..273W][1999ApJ...524..262M], and how the burst relates to the accompanying supernova explosion, a question crystallized by the spatial-temporal coincidence of the broad-lined Type Ic SN 1998bw with GRB 980425 [1998Natur.395..670G][1998Natur.395..672I][1998Natur.395..663K]. The community works out the progenitor channels (Wolf-Rayet collapse, helium-star/black-hole mergers, rotating low-metallicity single stars) [1993ApJ...405..273W][1998ApJ...502L...9F][2005A&A...443..643Y], the engine energetics (neutrino annihilation from a thick disk vs. rotating magnetized neutron stars) [1993ApJ...405..273W][1992Natur.357..472U][1996A&A...305..839J], and the observational GRB-SN connection and its exceptions [1998ApJ...504L..87W][2003Natur.423..847H][2006Natur.444.1047F][2022Natur.612..232Y].

## Populations and events

- SN 1998bw / GRB 980425 — the founding event: an unusually luminous, radio-relativistic Type Ic supernova (hypernova) coincident with an underluminous GRB [1998Natur.395..670G, 1998Natur.395..672I, 1998Natur.395..663K]
- SN 2003dh / GRB 030329 — the confirming spectroscopic association of a very energetic supernova with a cosmological GRB at z=0.1685 [2003Natur.423..847H]
- Type Ib/c supernovae as the GRB-producing population; Type Ia excluded at >4 sigma [1998ApJ...504L..87W]
- Long complex GRBs attributed to massive-star collapse; short hard GRBs attributed to compact mergers [1993ApJ...405..273W, 1999ApJ...524..262M, 1998ApJ...502L...9F]
- GRBs 970228/970508/970828 located near star-forming regions [1998ApJ...494L..45P]
- Supernova-less long bursts GRB 060505 and GRB 060614 [2006Natur.444.1047F]
- GRB 211211A — a long-duration burst with kilonova-like excess pointing to a compact-merger (possibly WD-NS) origin [2022Natur.612..232Y]
- Rotating Wolf-Rayet / helium-star progenitors, black holes of 2-3 solar masses with a~0.9, and hyperaccreting disks [1993ApJ...405..273W, 1999ApJ...524..262M]

## Instruments and datasets

- BATSE on the Compton Gamma Ray Observatory — spatial and luminosity distributions supporting cosmological distances [1992Natur.357..472U]
- Optical afterglow imaging/spectroscopy locating transients in host galaxies (ESO 184-G82 for SN 1998bw) [1998Natur.395..670G]
- Radio monitoring of SN 1998bw showing apparent superluminal expansion [1998Natur.395..663K]
- Optical spectroscopy and multiband photometry of GRB-supernovae (SN 1998bw, SN 2003dh) [1998Natur.395..672I, 2003Natur.423..847H]
- Catalogs of Type Ia and Ib/c supernova maximum dates cross-matched against GRB times (101 Ia + 17 Ib/c) [1998ApJ...504L..87W]
- The two-dimensional hydrodynamics code PROMETHEUS for collapsar simulations [1999ApJ...524..262M]
- Multiband optical/near-infrared follow-up of afterglows for supernova/kilonova searches [2006Natur.444.1047F, 2022Natur.612..232Y]

## Characteristic methods

- Analytical modeling of neutrino-cooled thick accretion disks and neutrino-annihilation energy deposition along the rotation axis [1993ApJ...405..273W, 1998ApJ...502L...9F, 1996A&A...305..839J]
- Multidimensional hydrodynamic simulation of collapsing rotating stellar cores, jet formation and jet breakout [1999ApJ...524..262M]
- Supernova light-curve and LTE/non-LTE spectral modeling to infer ejecta kinetic energies and progenitor masses [1998Natur.395..672I, 1999ApJ...516..788W]
- Radio light-curve analysis under synchrotron assumptions to infer relativistic expansion [1998Natur.395..663K]
- Statistical temporal/spatial cross-correlation of supernova samples with GRB positions and star-forming regions [1998ApJ...504L..87W, 1998ApJ...494L..45P]
- Rotating stellar-evolution calculations with magnetic angular-momentum transport and metallicity-dependent winds [2005A&A...443..643Y]
- Deep photometric supernova/kilonova searches in nearby GRB afterglows to test progenitor predictions [2006Natur.444.1047F, 2022Natur.612..232Y]

## Recurring models

- The collapsar / failed-supernova model: a rotating Wolf-Rayet or helium core collapses to a black hole plus thick accretion disk that powers a beamed relativistic jet [1993ApJ...405..273W, 1999ApJ...524..262M]
- The hypernova: an extremely energetic (~10^52 erg) explosion of a stripped carbon-oxygen star [1998Natur.395..672I, 1998ApJ...494L..45P, 1999ApJ...516..788W]
- Neutrino-annihilation pair fireballs above a hyperaccreting disk as the jet-launching mechanism [1993ApJ...405..273W, 1998ApJ...502L...9F, 1996A&A...305..839J]
- Magnetic extraction of black-hole rotational energy (Blandford-Znajek-type) as the alternative disk-engine channel [1998ApJ...494L..45P, 1998ApJ...502L...9F]
- The millisecond magnetar: a rapidly rotating neutron star with ~10^15 G field losing rotational energy in a relativistic wind [1992Natur.357..472U, 1998ApJ...504L..87W]
- Binary progenitor channels: helium star/black hole mergers via common-envelope evolution [1998ApJ...502L...9F]
- Chemically homogeneous evolution of rapidly rotating metal-poor single stars as a collapsar progenitor route [2005A&A...443..643Y]
- The long/short duration dichotomy mapped to collapse vs. compact-merger origins [1999ApJ...524..262M, 2022Natur.612..232Y]

## Results that shaped it

- Woosley's 1993 model showing a black-hole accretion disk from a collapsed rotating Wolf-Rayet star can deposit ~10^50 erg/s via neutrino annihilation, founding the collapsar paradigm [1993ApJ...405..273W]
- Discovery of the luminous Type Ic SN 1998bw in the error box of GRB 980425, with gamma-ray energy four orders of magnitude below other GRBs [1998Natur.395..670G]
- Light-curve modeling of SN 1998bw requiring ejecta kinetic energy of (2-5)x10^52 erg — the 'hypernova' — implying a new explosion mechanism [1998Natur.395..672I]
- Radio observations of SN 1998bw demanding apparent expansion at >=2c, i.e. a relativistic shock from a supernova [1998Natur.395..663K]
- MacFadyen & Woosley's 2D collapsar simulations: disk accretion at ~0.07 Msun/s for 10-20 s, jets beamed to ~1% of the sky penetrating the star in ~10 s, predicting collapsars always make SN 1998bw-like supernovae [1999ApJ...524..262M]
- Statistical exclusion of Type Ia supernovae as GRB sources (>4 sigma) and consistency of Type Ib/c [1998ApJ...504L..87W]
- The argument that GRBs lying in star-forming regions would force abandonment of the neutron-star-merger model for long bursts [1998ApJ...494L..45P]
- Spectroscopic confirmation via SN 2003dh/GRB 030329 that long GRBs signal the collapse of massive stars [2003Natur.423..847H]
- Discovery of two long GRBs (060505, 060614) with no supernova to limits hundreds of times fainter than SN 1998bw, breaking the universality of the GRB-SN connection [2006Natur.444.1047F]
- Yoon & Langer's demonstration that rapidly rotating metal-poor stars evolving chemically homogeneously satisfy collapsar requirements, tying GRB production to low metallicity [2005A&A...443..643Y]

## Internal disagreements

- White-dwarf collapse as a cosmological GRB source: Usov proposed accretion-induced collapse of magnetized white dwarfs to millisecond magnetars as the cosmological GRB engine [1992Natur.357..472U], while Woosley & Baron computed that the neutrino-driven wind in such a collapse (~0.005 Msun/s mass loss) obscures any prompt display and 'certainly rules out' cosmological GRBs from this channel [1992ApJ...391..228W]
- Energetics of neutron-star-merger disks: Woosley 1993 allowed NS mergers as one route to the GRB-powering black-hole disk [1993ApJ...405..273W], but Jaroszynski found that tori from NS-merger parameters give too little energy even with artificially high entropy and viscosity, favoring rotating Wolf-Rayet collapse instead [1996A&A...305..839J]
- One GRB class or two: Kulkarni et al. argued SN 1998bw establishes a second class of gamma-ray burst distinctly different from the cosmological kind [1998Natur.395..663K], whereas Woosley, Eastman & Schmidt modeled GRB 980425 as the same black-hole-accretion/jet engine seen with weaker or misaligned jets (Gamma~5 material along the line of sight), i.e. a continuum with ordinary bursts [1999ApJ...516..788W]; Wang & Wheeler went further, taking SN 1998bw-like Ib/c events as possibly the general GRB mechanism with ~10^-3 sr collimation [1998ApJ...504L..87W]
- Universality of the GRB-supernova connection: the collapsar simulations predicted that collapsars will always make supernovae similar to SN 1998bw [1999ApJ...524..262M], but GRB 060505 and GRB 060614 showed no supernova to limits hundreds of times fainter than SN 1998bw [2006Natur.444.1047F], and the long-duration GRB 211211A showed kilonova-like emission indicating a non-massive-star (compact-merger) origin, breaking the long-duration = collapsar mapping [2022Natur.612..232Y]
- Central engine identity: black-hole hyperaccretion with neutrino annihilation [1993ApJ...405..273W, 1999ApJ...524..262M] versus rapidly rotating, strongly magnetized neutron stars (millisecond magnetars) as the source of the relativistic outflow [1992Natur.357..472U, 1998ApJ...504L..87W]
- Progenitor channel: binary routes such as helium-star/black-hole mergers via common-envelope evolution [1998ApJ...502L...9F] versus single rapidly rotating metal-poor stars evolving chemically homogeneously, a channel argued to dominate at low metallicity [2005A&A...443..643Y]

## Activity

Founded in the early 1990s by theory papers on black-hole accretion disks and magnetar formation as cosmological GRB engines [1993ApJ...405..273W, 1992Natur.357..472U, 1992ApJ...391..228W]; exploded in 1998-1999 around SN 1998bw/GRB 980425 and the collapsar simulations [1998Natur.395..670G, 1999ApJ...524..262M]; consolidated in 2003-2007 with the SN 2003dh confirmation and progenitor stellar-evolution work [2003Natur.423..847H, 2005A&A...443..643Y]. With a median year of 2012 for its 1542 papers, the bulk of activity is post-Swift, and the community remains active into the 2020s, now driven by classification-breaking events such as GRB 211211A [2022Natur.612..232Y].
