# C7: GRB Luminosity Correlations and Cosmology

**Defining problem.** Whether empirical correlations between prompt-emission observables of gamma-ray bursts and their intrinsic luminosity or energy can turn GRBs into standardizable candles — first to assign distances to bursts without redshifts, then to extend the Hubble diagram beyond the supernova range and constrain dark energy. The founding move was the discovery that spectral lag anticorrelates with peak luminosity, L_53 ~ 1.3(tau/0.01 s)^-1.15 [2000ApJ...534..248N], paralleled by the luminosity-variability correlation [2001ApJ...552...57R] and the E_peak-E_iso (Amati) correlation found in twelve BeppoSAX bursts with redshifts [2002A&A...390...81A]. The community's defining tension is whether these relations are intrinsic physics or artifacts of detector thresholds and redshift-measurement selection [2005ApJ...627..319B, 2007ApJ...671..656B, 2013A&A...557A.100H].

## Populations and events

- Twelve BeppoSAX GRBs with known redshifts, the founding Amati-relation sample [2002A&A...390...81A]
- Six-seven BATSE/BeppoSAX bursts with redshifts plus the 174 brightest long BATSE bursts, the lag-luminosity samples [2000ApJ...534..248N]
- Twenty bursts from BATSE/Konus/Ulysses/NEAR defining the luminosity-variability relation [2001ApJ...552...57R]
- GRB 980425 / SN 1998bw, the persistent low-luminosity outlier that falls a factor of several hundred below the lag-luminosity power law [2000ApJ...534..248N] yet is consistent with the variability relation [2001ApJ...552...57R]
- 689 BATSE GRBs assigned pseudo-redshifts via the E_p-luminosity relation to trace the GRB formation rate to z~12 [2004ApJ...609..935Y]
- 17 cosmological GRBs with k-corrected bolometric energies, median E_iso = 2.19e53 erg [2001AJ....121.2879B]
- 12 GRBs with redshift, E_peak, and jet-break time used for the first Omega_M/dark-energy constraints [2004ApJ...612L.101D]
- 69 GRBs spanning 0.17 < z > 6 forming the largest GRB Hubble diagram of its era [2007ApJ...660...16S]
- 218 Swift BAT bursts (77 with redshift) used to argue the pre-Swift correlations are threshold artifacts [2007ApJ...671..656B]
- 43 Fermi GRBs with redshift vs 243 without, used to dissect the Amati plane into a physical boundary plus selection biases [2013A&A...557A.100H]

## Instruments and datasets

- CGRO/BATSE four-channel light curves and spectral catalogs [2000ApJ...534..248N, 1999ApJ...511..550L, 2005ApJ...627..319B]
- BeppoSAX 2-700 keV time-integrated spectra of redshift bursts [2002A&A...390...81A]
- Wind/Konus, Ulysses/GRB, and NEAR/XGRS light curves supplementing BATSE [2001ApJ...552...57R]
- Combined BeppoSAX + BATSE E_p and peak-luminosity data [2004ApJ...609..935Y]
- Swift/BAT complete spectral and duration catalog of 218 bursts [2007ApJ...671..656B]
- Fermi/GBM homogeneous spectral sample of 286 bursts [2013A&A...557A.100H]
- Cross-instrument BATSE/Swift/Suzaku/Konus fluence-E_peak comparisons [2012ApJ...747...39C]
- Host-galaxy R-band photometry used for redshift probability distributions [2001ApJ...561..171J]

## Characteristic methods

- Cross-correlation spectral-lag measurement between low and high energy bands [2000ApJ...534..248N]
- Rigorously constructed light-curve variability measures fit with models separating intrinsic and extrinsic scatter [2001ApJ...552...57R]
- Band-model spectral fitting and transformation to the cosmological rest frame [2002A&A...390...81A]
- Cosmological k-correction of fluences to a fixed comoving bandpass [2001AJ....121.2879B]
- Nonparametric statistical corrections for detector selection and data truncation [1999ApJ...511..550L, 2000ApJ...534..227L]
- Collimation correction of E_iso using achromatic afterglow jet breaks [2004ApJ...616..331G]
- Pseudo-redshift estimation for bursts without spectroscopic distances via the E_p-luminosity relation [2004ApJ...609..935Y]
- Combining multiple luminosity indicators into a GRB Hubble diagram and chi-square fitting to dark-energy models with w(z) parameterizations [2007ApJ...660...16S, 2004ApJ...612L.101D]
- Monte Carlo simulations of detector-threshold selection effects to test whether correlations are artifacts [2005ApJ...627..319B, 2007MNRAS.379L..55L, 2012ApJ...747...39C]

## Recurring models

- The collimated relativistic jet, with achromatic afterglow breaks giving opening angles and beaming-corrected energies E_gamma [2004ApJ...616..331G, 2004ApJ...612L.101D]
- Viewing-angle kinematics: off-axis jets produce dim, long-lag, low-variability bursts, unifying the lag-luminosity and variability relations and explaining GRB 980425 and X-ray-rich events [2001ApJ...554L.163I]
- Internal vs external shock synchrotron emission, with the E_p-fluence correlation favoring internal shocks [2000ApJ...534..227L]
- GRBs as standardizable candles calibrated by luminosity relations, in analogy to Cepheids [2001ApJ...552...57R, 2007ApJ...660...16S]
- Lognormal isotropic-energy distributions with mean ~1.3e53 erg [2001ApJ...561..171J]
- Flat LambdaCDM concordance cosmology as the fitting framework, with GRBs testing constant vs evolving dark energy w [2007ApJ...660...16S, 2004ApJ...612L.101D]
- GRB formation rate tracking the cosmic star formation rate at z<2 [2004ApJ...609..935Y], against earlier evidence that the GRB rate density differs from the SFR [1999ApJ...511..550L]

## Results that shaped it

- The lag-luminosity anticorrelation L_53 ~ 1.3(tau/0.01 s)^-1.15 in bursts with redshifts, founding the luminosity-indicator program [2000ApJ...534..248N]
- The luminosity-variability correlation L ~ V^3.3, an explicitly Cepheid-like luminosity estimator accurate to a factor ~4 [2001ApJ...552...57R]
- The Amati correlation: E_iso correlates with rest-frame E_peak in twelve BeppoSAX bursts, with more distant bursts systematically harder [2002A&A...390...81A]
- Demonstration that the E_p-fluence correlation cannot be produced by cosmological expansion alone and must be intrinsic, favoring internal shocks [2000ApJ...534..227L]
- The Ghirlanda relation: collimation-corrected E_gamma correlates tightly with E_peak, E_peak(1+z) ~ E_gamma^0.7, immediately proposed for cosmology [2004ApJ...616..331G]
- First GRB dark-energy constraints from the tight E_gamma-E_p relation: Omega_M = 0.35 +/- 0.15 and w = -0.84, consistent with Type Ia supernovae [2004ApJ...612L.101D]
- The Yonetoku E_p-peak-luminosity relation and pseudo-redshifts for 689 BATSE bursts, giving a GRB formation rate correlated with star formation at z<=2 and roughly constant to z~12 [2004ApJ...609..935Y]
- The 69-burst GRB Hubble diagram to z>6 combining five luminosity indicators, finding concordance LambdaCDM consistent at rms scatter 0.65 mag, only a factor 2 worse than supernova surveys [2007ApJ...660...16S]
- The counterattack: 88% of BATSE bursts are inconsistent with the Amati relation, implying it is a selection artifact [2005ApJ...627..319B], and the Swift catalog showing pre-Swift correlations inconsistent at >5 sigma and partially correlated with detector threshold [2007ApJ...671..656B]
- The k-correction methodology showing uncorrected energies undercount bolometric output by 5-600%, standardizing all subsequent energetics work [2001AJ....121.2879B]

## Internal disagreements

- Intrinsic physics vs selection artifact — the community's central war. Amati et al. reported the E_p-E_iso correlation while acknowledging possible truncation biases [2002A&A...390...81A], and Lloyd et al. argued the E_p-fluence correlation must be intrinsic [2000ApJ...534..227L]; Band & Preece countered that 88% of BATSE bursts violate the Amati relation and about a third violate the Ghirlanda relation, making them sample artifacts [2005ApJ...627..319B]; Butler et al. found the Swift correlation inconsistent with the pre-Swift one at >5 sigma and arising artificially from the detector threshold [2007ApJ...671..656B]; Heussaff et al. attempted a synthesis with Fermi data: a real physical lower-right boundary in the E_p-E_iso plane plus redshift-measurement selection producing the apparent tightness [2013A&A...557A.100H].
- Whether GRBs may be used for cosmology at all: Dai et al. derived Omega_M and w from the Ghirlanda relation [2004ApJ...612L.101D] and Schaefer built a 69-burst Hubble diagram concluding the concordance model fits [2007ApJ...660...16S], while Butler et al. declared the pre-Swift correlations 'likely useless for tests of cosmology' [2007ApJ...671..656B] and Collazzi et al. concluded the Amati relation 'should not be used for cosmological purposes' — while explicitly exempting the other luminosity relations [2012ApJ...747...39C].
- The status of GRB 980425/SN 1998bw: it falls below the lag-luminosity relation by a factor of several hundred [2000ApJ...534..248N], but its luminosity and variability are consistent with the L-V fit, suggesting a common origin with cosmological bursts [2001ApJ...552...57R], and viewing-angle jet kinematics can reproduce the lag-luminosity relation including 980425 as a large-viewing-angle event [2001ApJ...554L.163I].
- Redshift evolution of the relations: Yonetoku et al. assumed a universal E_p-luminosity relation to derive pseudo-redshifts [2004ApJ...609..935Y], whereas Li found the Amati parameters vary systematically with redshift with only ~4% probability of being a fluence-limit selection effect, implying strong evolution that would bias any uncorrected cosmological use [2007MNRAS.379L..55L].
- GRB rate vs star formation rate: the E_p-luminosity pseudo-redshift analysis found the GRB rate well correlated with the SFR at z<=2 [2004ApJ...609..935Y], while the earlier fluence-based nonparametric study found a GRB comoving rate density quite different from the star formation rate [1999ApJ...511..550L].

## Activity

Founded 1999-2002 with the lag-luminosity, variability, and Amati relations [2000ApJ...534..248N, 2001ApJ...552...57R, 2002A&A...390...81A]; peak construction phase 2004-2007 when the Ghirlanda and Yonetoku relations were converted into dark-energy constraints and Hubble diagrams [2004ApJ...616..331G, 2004ApJ...612L.101D, 2004ApJ...609..935Y, 2007ApJ...660...16S], immediately shadowed by the selection-effects counterattack [2005ApJ...627..319B, 2007ApJ...671..656B]. The median year of 2013 reflects the long adjudication phase with Swift and Fermi samples [2012ApJ...747...39C, 2013A&A...557A.100H]; the field remains active in cosmology-tension work, e.g. GRB-augmented high-redshift Hubble diagrams testing flat LambdaCDM [2019A&A...628L...4L], but the correlation-discovery era is over.
