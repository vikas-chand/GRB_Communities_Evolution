# C1: GRB Afterglow Fireball Physics

**Defining problem.** What powers gamma-ray bursts and their multiwavelength afterglows, and at what distance? The community formed around the relativistic fireball external-shock idea — that a fireball's expansion energy is reconverted to radiation when it decelerates in an external medium [1992MNRAS.258P..41R] — which predicted long-lived optical, X-ray, and radio emission fading as power laws [1997ApJ...476..232M; 1993ApJ...418L...5P]. The 1997 BeppoSAX-enabled discoveries of the first X-ray afterglow [1997Natur.387..783C], the first optical transient [1997Natur.386..686V], the first redshift [1997Natur.387..878M], and the first radio afterglow [1997Natur.389..261F] converted these predictions into an observational program: use afterglow spectra and light curves to test blast-wave physics and infer burst energetics, geometry, and environments [1997MNRAS.288L..51W; 1998ApJ...497L..17S; 1999ApJ...519L..17S].

## Populations and events

- GRB 970228 — first X-ray afterglow (8 hr after burst, power-law decay) and first fading optical transient associated with a faint galaxy [1997Natur.387..783C; 1997Natur.386..686V]
- GRB 970508 — first redshift limit (0.835 <= z <~ 2.3 from absorption spectroscopy) and first radio afterglow, with scintillation giving a ~3 microarcsecond source size [1997Natur.387..878M; 1997Natur.389..261F]
- GRB 970402 — early X-ray/optical afterglow used with 970228 to constrain fireball-model parameters [1997ApJ...485L...5W]
- GRB 980519 — rapid t^(-2.05±0.04) optical/X-ray decay taken as evidence for a jet [1999ApJ...519L..17S]
- GRBs 990123 and 990510 — afterglows favoring constant-density interstellar interaction [2000ApJ...536..195C]
- GRB 980425/SN 1998bw and GRBs 980326, 980519 — afterglows explained by stellar-wind interaction, tying bursts to massive-star progenitors [2000ApJ...536..195C]
- The long-burst population as a whole, whose isotropic sky distribution but inhomogeneous spatial distribution framed the distance-scale problem [1997Natur.387..783C; 1990ApJ...348..485P]
- The bimodal burst-duration distribution, tentatively attributed to Newtonian vs relativistic reverse shocks [1995ApJ...455L.143S]

## Instruments and datasets

- BeppoSAX (SAX) satellite — Wide Field Cameras for rapid localization and narrow-field X-ray follow-up; the PDS high-energy instrument (15-300 keV, 800 cm^2 phoswich) with CsI shields doubling as a GRB monitor [1991AdSpR..11h.281F; 1997Natur.387..783C; 1997MNRAS.288L..51W]
- Ground-based optical telescopes performing rapid error-box imaging within ~21 hours of a burst [1997Natur.386..686V]
- Optical spectrographs delivering absorption-line redshifts of afterglow transients [1997Natur.387..878M]
- Radio interferometric monitoring of afterglows, including interstellar-scintillation 'twinkling' as a size probe [1997Natur.389..261F; 1993ApJ...418L...5P]
- Multiwavelength (gamma-ray to radio) follow-up campaigns triggered by rapid burst localizations [1997Natur.387..783C; 1997Natur.389..261F]

## Characteristic methods

- Analytical relativistic blast-wave / fireball shock dynamics for energy conversion timescales and deceleration [1992MNRAS.258P..41R; 1995ApJ...455L.143S]
- Synchrotron spectral and light-curve modeling with power-law electron distributions, yielding explicit spectral-index/temporal-index relations (closure relations) [1998ApJ...497L..17S; 1997ApJ...485L...5W]
- Power-law fitting of multiwavelength afterglow decays to test blast-wave predictions [1997Natur.387..783C; 1997MNRAS.288L..51W]
- Rapid multi-wavelength follow-up of arcminute localizations, using positional coincidence to chain gamma-ray, X-ray, optical, and radio counterparts [1997Natur.386..686V; 1997Natur.389..261F]
- Absorption-line spectroscopy plus Lyman-alpha-forest arguments to bracket afterglow redshifts [1997Natur.387..878M]
- Interstellar scintillation analysis to measure afterglow angular sizes and expansion [1997Natur.389..261F]
- Discrimination of circumburst density profiles (r^-2 wind vs constant ISM) from afterglow evolution, especially radio [2000ApJ...536..195C]
- Statistical tests of angular and brightness distributions (dipole/quadrupole moments, V/Vmax) against galactic source models [1990ApJ...348..485P]

## Recurring models

- Relativistic fireball with external shock deceleration in ambient medium [1992MNRAS.258P..41R; 1997MNRAS.288L..51W]
- Synchrotron emission from shock-accelerated power-law electrons, with adiabatic and fully radiative limiting hydrodynamic evolutions [1998ApJ...497L..17S]
- Internal shocks within the ejecta shell for burst variability and precursors; reverse shocks (Newtonian vs relativistic) for duration structure [1995ApJ...455L.143S]
- Collimated jet models with lateral spreading, steepening decays and relaxing energy budgets by factors of hundreds [1999ApJ...519L..17S]
- Wind interaction models (Wolf-Rayet r^-2 wind) vs constant-density interstellar interaction, mapped to massive-star vs compact-merger progenitors [2000ApJ...536..195C]
- Inverse Compton emission dominating early afterglow and explaining delayed GeV photons [1997ApJ...485L...5W]
- Cosmological vs galactic-halo source populations as competing distance-scale frameworks [1997Natur.386..686V; 1990ApJ...348..485P]

## Results that shaped it

- Prediction that fireball energy is reconverted to radiation on second timescales when Lorentz-factor >~1000 ejecta hit an external medium — the theoretical charter of the field [1992MNRAS.258P..41R]
- Prediction of hours-long optical and days-to-weeks radio afterglows decaying as power laws, made before any afterglow was seen [1997ApJ...476..232M; 1993ApJ...418L...5P]
- First detection of an X-ray afterglow (GRB 970228), fading as a power law and carrying a significant fraction of the burst energy [1997Natur.387..783C]
- First fading optical transient, associated with a faint galaxy and pointing to cosmological distances [1997Natur.386..686V]
- First direct distance limit: absorption system at z=0.835 toward GRB 970508, proving at least some bursts are cosmological and highly energetic [1997Natur.387..878M]
- First radio afterglow (GRB 970508), with scintillation damping demonstrating source expansion [1997Natur.389..261F]
- Month-long power-law afterglow of GRB 970228 matching relativistic blast-wave predictions, implying ~10^51 erg and z~1 [1997MNRAS.288L..51W]
- Quantitative confrontation of the fireball model with GRB 970228/970402 data: electron index -2.3±0.1, t^-1 flux decline, xi_e <= 0.6 [1997ApJ...485L...5W]
- The standard broken-power-law synchrotron spectrum and light-curve framework with spectral-temporal closure relations [1998ApJ...497L..17S]
- Evidence from rapid afterglow decays that GRB outflows are jets, cutting energy requirements by factors of several hundred [1999ApJ...519L..17S]
- Internal-shock/deceleration timescale framework linking burst duration and variability to the shell hydrodynamics [1995ApJ...455L.143S]
- The BeppoSAX instrument suite whose localizations made the entire afterglow program possible [1991AdSpR..11h.281F]

## Internal disagreements

- Distance scale (the founding dispute): a countervailing view held that bursts were relatively local objects in a very large galactic halo, against the cosmological interpretation of their isotropic-but-inhomogeneous distribution [1997Natur.386..686V; 1997Natur.387..783C]; galactic neutron-star models were being quantitatively tested and partly ruled out [1990ApJ...348..485P] before the z=0.835 absorption system toward GRB 970508 settled the question in favor of cosmological distances [1997Natur.387..878M]
- Circumburst environment: standard afterglow modeling assumed a constant-density external medium [1998ApJ...497L..17S; 1997ApJ...485L...5W], whereas wind-interaction analysis argued GRBs 970228 and 970508 decelerate in a Wolf-Rayet r^-2 wind while 990123 and 990510 favor interstellar interaction — proposing two progenitor types rather than one medium [2000ApJ...536..195C]
- Outflow geometry and energetics: spherical blast-wave fits gave total energies of order 10^51 erg [1997MNRAS.288L..51W], but rapid afterglow decays such as GRB 980519's t^-2.05 were argued to be inconsistent with spherical (isotropic) models and to require laterally spreading jets, reducing the energy budget by factors of several hundred [1999ApJ...519L..17S]
- Shock hydrodynamics: whether afterglow evolution is fully adiabatic or fully radiative was posed as an open, observationally decidable dichotomy within the synchrotron framework [1998ApJ...497L..17S]

## Activity

Rooted in early-1990s fireball theory [1992MNRAS.258P..41R; 1993ApJ...418L...5P] and instrument building [1991AdSpR..11h.281F], the community ignited in 1997-2000 with the BeppoSAX afterglow discoveries and the model-testing wave that followed [1997Natur.387..783C; 1998ApJ...497L..17S; 2000ApJ...536..195C]. A median year of 2006 across 2136 papers places its peak in the Swift era of routine afterglow follow-up (dark-burst classification work continued through 2004-2011 [2004ApJ...617L..21J; 2011A&A...526A..30G]), with the framework still in active use into the 2010s, e.g. for the X-ray counterpart of GW170817 [2017Natur.551...71T]; the foundational questions are settled and current activity applies the toolkit rather than contesting it.
